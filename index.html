The problem

Everyone measures what was delivered. Almost nobody measures what changed.

The same failure in three costumes. In each case what gets counted is the effort, not the effect.

Product

You shipped the feature

Usage is up and funnel metrics moved. Whether people behave differently, or a number responded to something else entirely, is unresolved.

Missing: did behaviour change, or did a metric?
Platform

You rolled out the platform

Licences issued, dashboards built, governance published. Nobody can show that a single decision is now made differently because of it.

Missing: does anyone work or decide differently?
Programme

You delivered the programme

Attendance, completion and satisfaction all look healthy. None of it answers the question the person who approved the budget is asking.

Missing: is anything different afterwards?

The data industry sells transformation and measures installation. Adoption is the most under-measured thing in it, and adoption is a behaviour problem wearing a technical costume.

Why us

Statistics

Behaviour

A doctorate in behaviour change and Chartered Psychologist status. We know what a construct is, whether your metric captures it, and what actually moves human behaviour as opposed to what moves a number.

Data

An analyst can tell you what the data says. A statistician can tell you whether to believe it. A behavioural scientist can tell you what people will do about it. The audit buys the person who answers all three at once, which is cheaper than finding out later that you measured the wrong thing.

The engagement

Behaviour Impact Audit

One initiative. A shipped feature, a platform rollout, an experiment programme, a training or change programme. Not a whole analytics function.

£4,000 FIXED · 3 TO 5 DAYS · DELIVERED IN 3 WEEKS

What you provide

  • Three to five conversations, about forty minutes each: whoever built it, whoever measures it, whoever decides on it
  • What already exists on paper: metric definitions, a dashboard screenshot, experiment write-ups, the last report produced

What you receive

  • A written report, eight to twelve pages: what you can claim, what you cannot, where measurement breaks, what to fix first
  • A recorded walkthrough, ten to fifteen minutes, so it can be forwarded to people who were not in the room
  • One ninety-minute working session

Not included: access to or reanalysis of your data, building instruments, running new collection, dashboard work, ongoing advice. No data access means no data processing agreement, no security review, and we can start next week.

What follows: if the break is at capture or consequence, an evaluation build. If it is at belief or action, that is different work and we will say so rather than sell you the bigger thing.
